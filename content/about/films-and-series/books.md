## Sapiens: A Brief History of Humankind
Author: Yuval Noah Harari
Year: 2011
Why I like it: I love stories, and Harari is exceptionally good at telling them. Sapiens made human history feel less like a timeline of events and more like one enormous story we collectively agreed to believe in. I think that idea stayed with me more than any individual fact from the book.

## Homo Deus: A Brief History of Tomorrow
Author: Yuval Noah Harari
Year: 2015
Why I like it: Again, it’s the storytelling for me. Harari takes huge, abstract questions about technology, humanity and the future and somehow makes them feel like a story you want to keep following. I don’t necessarily agree with every conclusion, but I love the way he makes me think about what might come next.
Poster: posters/homo-deus.jpg
Spine: #151515 #e0452f
Size: 24 × 3.6

## 21 Lessons for the 21st Century
Author: Yuval Noah Harari
Year: 2018
Why I like it: The idea that stayed with me is that learning doesn’t really end anymore. The world changes too quickly for that. Being able to adapt, question what you know and learn again might be more important than knowing the “right” thing in the first place.

## The Little Prince
Author: Antoine de Saint-Exupéry
Year: 1943
Why I like it: I remember this book less through its plot and more through a very specific memory: lying in my room as a child and reading it. I still remember how it made me feel. Somehow that feeling survived much longer than most things I read back then.

I even have the Fox tattooed on me now. So I guess some stories really do stay.
Poster: posters/the-little-prince.jpeg
Spine: #3d5fb2 #fbf6e4
Size: 20 × 1.4

## Animal Farm
Author: George Orwell
Year: 1945
Why I like it: I wish this book felt more fictional than it does.

What I like about Animal Farm is how something so simple can describe patterns you keep recognising in real life: power, language, manipulation, and how easily the rules can change when the people writing them are the ones in control.
Poster: posters/animal-farm.jpg
Spine: #b1271f #f1e3c2
Size: 18 × 1.3

## Monday Begins on Saturday
Author: Arkady & Boris Strugatsky
Year: 1965
Why I like it: A programmer walks into a research institute where magic is treated like science. That alone was probably enough to get me.

I studied physics, so there’s something very familiar about its strange mix of researchers, experiments, bureaucracy and complete absurdity. Except here, the impossible is just another thing someone needs to investigate.
Poster: posters/monday-begins-on-saturday.jpeg
Spine: #c23a25 #111111
Size: 20 × 2.2

## Rough Draft
Author: Sergei Lukyanenko
Year: 2005
Why I like it: I’ve always loved stories about other worlds. But this one felt different because the person discovering them isn’t an eleven-year-old chosen one. He’s an adult whose perfectly ordinary life suddenly stops being ordinary.

I also can’t separate this book from Kraków. I was living there at the time, and we used to walk around the old quarries while listening to the audiobook. So now, in my head, those other worlds somehow have a little bit of Kraków in them too.

Spine: #1c3440 #efe5cf
Size: 21 × 3
