## Sleepy Hollow
Year: 1999
Why I like it: I love Tim Burton films in general, but Sleepy Hollow has a special place for me.

The darkness, the fog, the strange trees, the costumes, Christina Ricci as Katrina, the almost theatrical feeling of the whole world. It’s one of those films where I could probably turn the story off and still keep watching just for the atmosphere.
Poster: posters/sleepy-hollow-1999.jpg

## Van Helsing (2004)
Year: 2004
Why I like it: I’ve always loved the world behind Van Helsing almost as much as the film itself. Monsters, legends, Dracula, secret orders and a much bigger mythology than we ever got to see on screen.

I’m still a little sad that this universe never really continued.

And Anna Valerious made it even more personal for me. I have Roma ancestry, so as a kid it was nice to see a heroine from that world in the middle of a huge fantasy adventure. There was finally someone I could recognise a little bit of myself in.
Poster: posters/van-helsing-2004.jpg

## It (1990)
Year: 1990
Why I like it: This was my first real horror film.

And no, it wasn’t the new Pennywise that scared me. It was Tim Curry’s Pennywise from the 1990 version. He terrified me as a kid, and I’m pretty sure he’s at least partly responsible for my lifelong distrust of clowns.

I liked the newer films for a different reason. The kids actually feel like a group of real friends. Their chemistry, jokes and fear make the story work for me just as much as the horror.
Poster: posters/it-1990.jpeg

## Once Bitten
Year: 1985
Why I like it: And obviously, there had to be some Jim Carrey here.

Once Bitten is silly, weird and very 80s, but that’s exactly part of its charm. A teenage Jim Carrey, a vampire countess and a completely ridiculous story that somehow ends up being sweet and funny.

It’s not a film I’d try to defend as a masterpiece. I just genuinely enjoy it. Sometimes that’s enough.
Poster: posters/once-bitten.jpeg

## Constantine
Year: 2005
Why I like it: Demons, angels, mythology, ancient rules and a hidden supernatural world existing right next to the ordinary one. Of course I was going to like Constantine.

I’ve always been fascinated by stories that suggest our world is only one layer of something much stranger underneath.
Poster: posters/constantine.webp

## Bridget Jones's Diary
Year: 2001
Why I like it: What can I say? It’s simple, warm and familiar.

For me, Bridget Jones is one of those films that doesn’t need a special reason to watch. Give me a cold autumn evening, a blanket and Bridget making questionable life decisions, and I’m happy.
Poster: posters/bridget-joness-diary.jpg

## The Mummy
Year: 1999
Why I like it: I love pretty much the whole franchise. Like many kids who grew up in the 90s, I watched The Mummy and immediately thought archaeology looked like the best job in the world.

Now I’m waiting for the next film in 2027. I just hope they don’t ruin it. I’m still recovering from the last Indiana Jones.
Poster: posters/the-mummy-v2.jpeg

## The Lord of the Rings: The Fellowship of the Ring
Year: 2001
Why I like it: One of my earliest memories of fantasy is watching The Lord of the Rings with my dad. I remember how much he wanted to see it, which made me want to see it too.

I also remember being genuinely terrified when Gandalf fell in Moria.

Years later, I still love this world, but the Shire most of all. If someone offered me a small Hobbit house, a garden and absolutely no urgent Slack messages, I think I’d manage.
Poster: posters/lotr-fellowship.jpg

## Balto
Year: 1995
Why I like it: I’ve always had a soft spot for characters who start from the bottom, especially the ones nobody really expects much from.

That’s probably why Balto stayed with me. He doesn’t begin as the obvious hero. He has to become one.

Apparently I’ve been rooting for underdogs for quite a long time.
Poster: posters/balto.webp

## Mulan
Year: 1998
Why I like it: I loved that Mulan didn’t wait for someone to decide she could be a hero.

She walked into a world where she wasn’t supposed to belong, learned how it worked and proved that she could do it her own way.

I think that part of the story stayed with me much longer than I realised.
Poster: posters/mulan.jpeg
